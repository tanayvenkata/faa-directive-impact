## Questions and Answers (summarized)

| Question | FAA response | Cases |
|---|---|---|
| A listed hub well below its limit; the qualifying shop visit is 40 FC after the effective date. Must the hub be removed at that visit? | No. The hub is not removed at a shop visit inside the first 100 FC; it is removed at its removal cycle limit (3,900 CSN in the example). | 005 |
| A hub with a 100 CSN limit, at 60 CSN on the effective date, and no qualifying shop visit. May it pass 100 CSN? | Yes. It must be removed before 160 CSN, which is 100 FC after the effective date. | 006 |

Stated intent: the 100 flight cycles is a drawdown that lets parts already past
their removal cycle limit keep flying briefly instead of grounding the engine
immediately. A remark that "the 100 CSN part" was an exception was retracted by
the sender in a follow-up the same day.

## Consequence

Neither grammatical reading recorded in seed-005 (A: remove within 100 FC; B:
remove at the shop visit) matched the FAA's intent. The safe behavior required
by the seed, which was to route to review without choosing a reading, was
correct. When the first qualifying shop visit is inside the 100-FC window, the
reference derivation applies the FAA reading: latest = max(limit point, 100 FC
after the effective date).
